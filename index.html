The loop

Acquire, convert, understand, retain, compound.

This is the shape of every engagement, whether the deliverable this month is a campaign, a storefront or a tracking fix. Skip a stage and the next one gets more expensive to run.

01

Acquire

Meta and Google, run against a break-even ROAS derived from the margins, not an industry average.

02

Convert

Storefront and checkout built to take the order without friction — catalogue, pricing architecture, guest checkout, payment gateway.

03

Understand

GA4, Tag Manager and Meta Pixel reconciled against the payment gateway, because a Pixel that fires is not the same as a Pixel that is correct.

04

Retain

Post-purchase sequences and pricing that make the second order cheaper to win than the first.

05

Compound

Contribution margin gets reinvested into acquisition, and the same loop runs faster the next time round.

Selected work

Al Aziz Perfumes

Offline to D2C 14 SKUs 2026 alazizparfums.com ↗

A single-store perfume retailer in Navi Mumbai with no online presence, selling at ₹450 and making ₹100 a unit. At that margin his break-even ROAS was 4.5x, which no one reliably hits on cold traffic — the business was not ad-viable before a rupee had been spent. So the first deliverable was not a campaign. It was a price.

I took the rest of it too: storefront, measurement, acquisition plan, organic social and every creative asset — sole operator, start to finish.

The positioning came out of the benchmark rather than a brainstorm. Fourteen eau de parfums at one flat price, no inflated MRP, no theatre. In a category built on discounting off an imaginary list price, refusing to play that game is the differentiation.

Set at launch from the unit model. These are targets to clear, not outcomes achieved.

01

Repriced it before writing a single ad

A GST-corrected unit model replaced the ₹450 price with a ₹799 launch price, moving contribution margin from 22% to 55% and break-even ROAS from 4.5x to 1.83x. Two- and three-bottle bundles lift contribution per order to ₹786 and ₹1,136 without raising acquisition cost. Everything downstream depended on this being right first.

02

Built the storefront

WooCommerce end to end. Fourteen-SKU catalogue, pricing architecture, guest checkout and Razorpay integration. Live and taking orders.

03

Wired the measurement

GA4, Google Tag Manager and Meta Pixel with Conversions API. Purchase events reconciled against the payment gateway rather than assumed, because a Pixel that fires is not the same as a Pixel that is correct.

04

Benchmarked the category

Four competing fragrance brands compared on price per ml, AOV and positioning. That set the price band and pointed to a craftsman-perfumer story rather than another discount-led entrant.

05

Designed the acquisition plan

A ₹15,000 per month Meta budget structured against category benchmarks, fronted by a ₹599 discovery-set offer with a coupon-driven trial-to-full-bottle funnel.

06

Ran the organic side

Full ownership of organic social, warming an audience for a brand starting from no digital presence at all, so the paid account was not the only thing carrying the launch.

07

Made every creative asset

Creative strategy and production in-house, using Gemini and Canva. Positioning came out of the benchmark, and the assets were built to carry it rather than decorate it.

08

Closed the leaks before opening the tap

Prepaid-only at launch, removing roughly ₹140 of loss per failed delivery, plus a seven-touch WhatsApp post-purchase sequence to make the second order cheaper than the first.

Where this stands. The store is live and the campaign is running. Spend is still accumulating, so there is no ROAS figure on this page yet. When there is one it goes here whether or not it flatters me. Numbers you cannot check are worth nothing, and most of the ones on sites like this are exactly that.

How I work

Fix the data. Then scale the spend.

The order matters. Spend optimising toward broken events does not merely waste budget, it teaches the algorithm the wrong lesson and the damage compounds. So measurement gets fixed first, every time, including the engagements where it is the least interesting part of the work.

I came to this from sales rather than from design, which means I think in contribution margin before I think in creative. I would rather show you a pricing model than a mood board. If the number does not move, it was decoration.

I work with a small number of brands at a time and I document what I learn in public, including the parts that did not work. There is a great deal of confident nonsense in this field and most of it is generated rather than earned.
